Practice Plan 01

Ethan's Easy First Tennis Lesson

45 MinutesAges 5–8Red BallBeginner

Purpose: Help a first-time player experience the basic flow of tennis through movement, ball control, hitting, rallying, and playing a simple game.

Move→Control→Hit→Rally→Play

The goal is not technical perfection. The goal is for the child to leave feeling:

“I can play tennis, and tennis is fun.”
Four cues for the whole lesson
Ready→Move→Hit→Recover

Keep language simple. Advanced stroke terminology can wait.

0–5 min
Move

Move & Catch

Start without racquets.

  • Two-hand catch
  • Move left/right and catch
  • Bounce once and catch
  • Try to catch 5 in a row

Goal: Warm up, develop hand-eye coordination, and help the child feel comfortable.

Ethan's Tip

Keep instructions short. Get the child moving and succeeding immediately.

Watch Example
Hit, Catch and Throw · LET'S Tennis Australia
5–10 min
Control

Racquet & Ball Control

Ball Balance

Balance a red ball on the racquet and walk to a cone.

Bounce Down

Bounce the ball downward with the racquet. Try for 5.

Bounce Up

Tap the ball gently upward.

Do not overemphasize grip technique during this first experience unless the grip creates an obvious problem.

Goal: Become comfortable controlling the racquet and ball.

Watch Example
Walking the Dog Course (Red Ball Kids Tennis) · Tennis Coaches To Coaches
10–18 min
Hit

Forehand Drop-Hit

Drop→Bounce→Hit

Simple cues

  • Turn sideways
  • Drop the ball in front
  • Let it bounce
  • Swing forward
  • Finish

First goal: Get the ball over the net. Once successful, add large cone targets.

Ethan's Tip

Don't worry about hitting hard. Get the ball over the net first.

Watch Example
Tennis Forehand Drill For Kids · United States Tennis Association (USTA)
18–25 min
Hit

Backhand Drop-Hit

Use a two-handed backhand for young beginners.

Drop→Bounce→Hit

Simple cues

  • Two hands
  • Turn
  • Hit in front

Keep the focus on a successful first experience, not technical perfection.

Goal: Help the child understand how to hit a ball arriving on the backhand side.

Watch Example
Mini Tennis Red Backhand — Control and Hit Over Net · Be Gr8 at Tennis — Rob Cherry
25–33 min
Rally

Toss, Hit & Rally

  1. Helper tosses → child hits forehand
  2. Alternate easy forehand/backhand feeds
  3. Helper racquet feeds → child hits → helper catches
  4. Attempt a real mini-rally

Goal: Transition from isolated strokes to actually playing tennis with another person.

Challenge: “Can we get 3?” Celebrate even two consecutive successful hits as a rally.
Watch Example
Toss Hit Catch · USTA Florida
33–38 min
Start

Start the Point

“The serve starts the point.”

  1. Throw the ball over the net
  2. Simple overhead racquet tap
  3. If ready, try an easy toss and hit

Do not spend significant time on formal serve mechanics during the first lesson.

Goal: Understand how a tennis point begins.

For first-time players: The example shows several red-ball serve options. Try only the easiest option that produces success.
Watch Example
Red Ball Six Serve Options · Jenny Walls Robb
38–45 min
Play

Game: Race to 5

Give the child easy feeds and keep the game achievable.

1 point Ball over the net2 points Ball into a target area

Goal: Race to 5.

Challenge: Let the child try to “Beat Ethan” or beat the helper.

Finish: Celebrate one thing the child learned and one thing they enjoyed.

Watch Example
Mini Tennis Red Ball — Play Points from Forehand Feed · Be Gr8 at Tennis — Rob Cherry

Make It Easier → Find Success → Add a Challenge

The objective is a high success rate while gradually increasing the challenge.

If the child struggles
  • Feed slower or closer
  • Move closer to the net
  • Use toss-hit-catch
  • Simplify to bounce-and-catch
If the activity becomes easy
  • Move farther away
  • Add targets or movement
  • Hit consecutive balls
  • Add simple scoring

About Ethan's Tips: These are practical suggestions from a dedicated junior player who enjoys helping younger and first-time players. They are not presented as professional coaching certification.
