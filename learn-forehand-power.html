BODY POSITION

Coach’s cue

He asks the player to feel body weight move toward the ball and to prepare before it bounces. The first demonstration is a controlled feed, with time to set up.

Why it helps

Moving into a manageable ball helps direct the shot forward while keeping contact and the finish balanced.

Another coaching perspective

USTA guidance also emphasizes a coordinated “unit turn” of the hips and upper body, while forehand research describes racket speed as a coordinated result of pelvis, trunk, shoulder, and arm actions. These ideas complement forward drive: use it as a feel when time allows, alongside a coordinated turn and balanced recovery. USTA: unit turn ↗ · Forehand biomechanics review ↗

MOVEMENT & SPIN

Coach’s cue

In the moving drill, he cues: prepare, turn the shoulders, run, stop, then drive through the ball and recover. He then asks the player to keep the forward intent while adding spin and net clearance.

Why it helps

This progression links preparation and movement to contact, then adds a control goal: send the ball with shape and enough net clearance.

Another coaching perspective

USTA’s forehand guidance also connects a low-to-high racket path with topspin and recommends prioritizing control as players learn. That complements the drill’s focus on movement and forward intent: players can adjust their footwork and spin to the incoming ball and target. USTA: forehand by level and spin ↗

READ & ADAPT

Coach’s cue

Mouratoglou explains that a full preparation can make a player late against a fast, deep ball. He demonstrates a shorter take-back, then asks the player to read whether the incoming ball is fast or slow and adjust the swing length.

Why it helps

A compact preparation leaves more time to meet a quick ball in front and use its pace, instead of rushing a large backswing.

Another coaching perspective

Forehand research also describes coordination as adapting to incoming-ball speed and shot direction. That supports Mouratoglou’s read-and-adjust approach; when the ball gives more time, a fuller preparation remains a useful option. Reid, Elliott & Crespo, 2013 ↗

ACCELERATION

Coach’s cue

He asks the player to turn, let the hand stay relaxed, and accelerate through the ball. He also has her picture a higher arc over the net and says not to force the shot by trying to hit hard.

Why it helps

A relaxed arm makes it easier to accelerate the racket while maintaining a controllable swing path and useful net margin.

Another coaching perspective

USTA guidance also pairs a low-to-high path for topspin with racquet-face control and a grip that is not overly tight. This complements the relaxed-hand cue: a comfortable grip and coordinated movement support acceleration while helping the player manage direction and height. USTA: grip, control, and spin ↗ · Forehand biomechanics review ↗

TRY IT ON COURT

One variable at a time

Start with 6 comfortable feeds. On a slower ball, prepare early and notice whether a small forward move helps you send the ball deep with balance. Then take 6 quicker feeds with a shorter preparation. Keep the same target and compare contact quality, net margin, and recovery. Stop if you feel pain or lose control.
