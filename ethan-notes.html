Learning Note 01

Flat vs. Topspin Forehand

Green / Yellow Ball

A flatter forehand is best treated as a situational attacking variation—not the default rally forehand.

High + short + balancedFlatten more.
↔
Low, deep, stretched, or off-balanceUse more topspin.

When to Flatten More

  • The ball is at or above net height.
  • You can take the ball early and stay balanced.
  • You have time for compact preparation and small adjustment steps.
  • You want to attack a shorter or higher ball.

What “Flat” Really Means

Flat does not mean zero topspin or a completely horizontal swing. An aggressive flatter forehand usually still has some topspin—it is simply more forward-dominant and lower-trajectory than a normal rally ball.

Think: more forward and less upward, while still clearing the net with margin.

Technical Priorities

01

Prepare compactly. Turn early, but do not make the backswing large.

02

Adjust your spacing. Use many small steps so contact happens comfortably in front.

03

Stay stable. Build pace from rotation and sequencing—not only from the arm.

04

Extend toward the target. Avoid stopping the racquet or wrapping across the body too early.

A useful point pattern

Heavier topspin→Create a shorter or higher ball→Step in→Flatten the next forehand

The flatter forehand works best as a choice created by the previous ball—not as a shot to force from a difficult position.

Important Refinements

Do not set the racquet exactly at ball height.

The racquet generally still approaches from somewhat below the contact point to create spin and margin.

Getting lower is not the whole answer to a low ball.

Low balls usually need more upward swing component and more spin, which makes a truly flat shot less appropriate.

Do not swing literally straight through the ball.

A forward-dominant swing still needs shape. Trying to swing completely flat often sends the ball long or into the net.

Simple decision rule

High + short + balanced = flatten more.
Low, deep, stretched, or off-balance = use more topspin.
